# Review of the supplied Claude figure

The broad scenario is coherent: a cold, dry north-Georgia/Piedmont wedge beneath a strong northern high, a much warmer and moister Gulf/coastal warm sector, a sharp boundary near the fall line, and southeast flow above the cold dome. A strong jet above an inversion is distinct from surface gusts inside the cold pool. The [NWS 2011 Southeast CAD case study](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/6930) provides relevant mechanism context; it does not validate this fictional event.

The time and low pressure agree with the original scenario: 7 PM EST Wednesday December 16, 2026 is 00Z Thursday December 17, model hour 48, when the prescribed low is 947 mb. Landfall is six hours later at hour 54, 941 mb. The user's latest Quebec-high value is **1049 mb**, superseding the figure's 1046 mb.

Things to correct or qualify:

- **Gradient is not frontogenesis.** The right panel shades the magnitude of a spatial temperature gradient in °F per 10 miles. Horizontal kinematic frontogenesis is a rate of strengthening or weakening of a potential-temperature gradient, commonly shown in K per 100 km per 3 hours. A large gradient can be weakening; a smaller gradient can be strengthening. See the [MetPy definition](https://unidata.github.io/MetPy/latest/api/generated/metpy.calc.frontogenesis.html).
- **Check sampling coordinates before treating labels as inconsistent.** Macon is 59°F on the left and 51°F on the right; Augusta is 58°F and 48°F. The left panel labels airport stations (MCN/AGS), while the right labels cities. Airport versus city-center samples can legitimately differ across a sharp front. These values should match only if the coordinates, valid time and field are the same; the image alone does not establish that. Verify the source coordinates rather than treating the differences as rounding or definite mistakes.
- **Check the 16°F-per-10-mile peak from the source grid.** This is an extremely sharp shallow-front contrast. Verify the grid spacing, smoothing and derivative calculation; a smooth-looking plotted boundary alone does not establish the peak value. The fall-line corridor is a conceptual placement, not a fixed rule for every CAD event.
- **CSI is a hypothesis here.** A surface map cannot diagnose conditional symmetric instability or place its bands. Saturation, vertical thermal structure, momentum/shear and lift must be considered. The [NWS CSI discussion](https://www.weather.gov/lmk/winterpt2) and [1993 Southeast case analysis](https://www.weather.gov/bmx/winter_stormofthecentury) describe the required vertical context.
- **The heaviest-freezing-rain axis is not established.** Warm air above shallow subfreezing surface air is consistent with freezing rain, but the vertical warm/cold-layer depths and temperatures matter. The exact 40–80-mile offset, precipitation intensity and ice accumulation cannot be inferred from this surface field. See the [NWS precipitation-type explanation](https://www.weather.gov/ama/preciptypes).

The figure is useful as a conceptual scenario reference. Its unreconciled station-versus-city sampling and unverified band annotations should not be used as calibration data for the numerical CAD experiment. The supplied image is retained as `reference_claude.png`.
