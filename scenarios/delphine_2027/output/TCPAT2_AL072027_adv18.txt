DISCUSSION AND OUTLOOK
----------------------
At 500 PM EDT (2100 UTC), the center of Hurricane Delphine was
located near latitude 26.4 North, longitude 85.4 West. Delphine is
moving toward the north-northeast near 12 mph (19 km/h). A turn
toward the north with a significant increase in forward speed is
expected tonight and Saturday. On the forecast track, the center of
Delphine will reach the Florida Big Bend coast early Saturday
morning, then move rapidly northward across southwestern and central
Georgia Saturday, passing near or over the Atlanta metropolitan area
Saturday afternoon. Delphine is then expected to slow down over the
Tennessee and Ohio Valleys Sunday and Monday.

Maximum sustained winds are near 130 mph (215 km/h) with higher
gusts. Delphine is a major hurricane -- a category 4 on the
Saffir-Simpson Hurricane Wind Scale. Additional strengthening is
forecast before landfall, and Delphine is expected to be a dangerous
major hurricane when it reaches the coast. Because of its fast
forward motion, Delphine is expected to remain a hurricane well
inland over Georgia, and it is forecast to become post-tropical over
the southern Appalachians by Sunday morning.

Hurricane-force winds extend outward up to 70 miles (110 km) from the
center and tropical-storm-force winds extend outward up to 230 miles
(370 km).

The estimated minimum central pressure is 944 mb (27.88 inches).


HAZARDS AFFECTING LAND
----------------------
STORM SURGE:  The combination of a dangerous storm surge and the tide
will cause normally dry areas near the coast to be flooded by rising
waters moving inland from the shoreline. The water could reach the
following heights above ground somewhere in the indicated areas if
the peak surge occurs at the time of high tide...
  - Carrabelle to Suwannee River, FL...15-20 ft
  - Ochlockonee River to Carrabelle, FL...10-15 ft
  - Suwannee River to Yankeetown, FL...10-15 ft
  - Yankeetown to Chassahowitzka, FL...7-11 ft
  - Chassahowitzka to Anclote River, FL...5-8 ft
  - Anclote River to Middle of Longboat Key, FL, including Tampa
    Bay...4-7 ft
  - Indian Pass to Carrabelle, FL...4-7 ft
The deepest water will occur along the immediate coast near and to
the east of the landfall location, where the surge will be
accompanied by large and destructive waves.

WIND:  Hurricane conditions are expected within the Hurricane Warning
area along the coast beginning late tonight, with tropical storm
conditions arriving this evening. Because of Delphine's fast forward
speed, damaging and life-threatening hurricane-force wind gusts will
extend well inland over portions of north Florida and southern and
central Georgia, including the Atlanta metropolitan area, Saturday.
Widespread tree damage and prolonged power outages are likely.
Tropical storm conditions are expected in the Tropical Storm Warning
areas along the Florida, Georgia and South Carolina coasts Saturday.

RAINFALL:  Delphine is expected to produce rainfall totals of 5 to 10
inches with isolated totals around 15 inches across the Florida Big
Bend, Georgia, and the southern Appalachians through Sunday. This
rainfall will likely result in considerable and potentially
catastrophic flash and urban flooding, with significant river
flooding likely. Numerous landslides are possible in the steep
terrain of the southern Appalachians.

TORNADOES:  A few tornadoes are possible tonight and Saturday across
the Florida Peninsula, southern and eastern Georgia, and South
Carolina.

SURF:  Swells generated by Delphine are affecting much of the Gulf
Coast and will spread across the Florida Peninsula coast during the
next day or so. These swells are likely to cause life-threatening
surf and rip current conditions. Please consult products from your
local weather office.
