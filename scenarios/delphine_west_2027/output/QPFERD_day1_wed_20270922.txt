...THERE IS A SLIGHT RISK OF EXCESSIVE RAINFALL ACROSS PORTIONS OF
NORTH GEORGIA...THE SOUTH CAROLINA UPSTATE...AND EAST-CENTRAL
ALABAMA...

...Southern Appalachians and Southeast...

A Slight Risk was introduced from east-central Alabama through the
Atlanta metro and into the South Carolina Upstate, with a surrounding
Marginal Risk. A quasi-stationary front draped from the Tennessee
Valley into the western Carolinas will become the focus for repeated
rounds of heavy rain today and tonight as deep tropical moisture
streams north well ahead of Hurricane Delphine over the northwestern
Caribbean. This is the early stage of what is increasingly likely to
become a multi-day predecessor rain event (PRE).

PWs of 2.0 to 2.3 inches, near the 97th percentile for late
September, will pool along the boundary. The mean 850-300 mb flow
will be nearly parallel to the front, which favors training. The 00Z
HREF paints a narrow corridor of 2 to 4 inches with localized 5 inch
amounts along and just north of I-85, and hourly rates of 1 to 2
inches are possible. Soils are near normal and 3-hour FFG is
generally 2.5 to 3.5 inches, so today's risk is mostly tied to urban
areas and repeated rounds over the same locations.

Of greater concern is the trend. Guidance keeps the front nearly in
place through Friday, and WPC is already carrying a Moderate Risk in
the Day 2 outlook for Thursday. Rain falling today will steadily
lower flash flood guidance ahead of Delphine's arrival late in the
week.
