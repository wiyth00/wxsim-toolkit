...North Georgia, East Alabama, and the Southern Appalachians...

A High Risk was issued across north Georgia, including metro Atlanta,
and east-central Alabama in coordination with WFOs FFC, BMX, GSP, and
MRX and the Southeast River Forecast Center. After three days of the
predecessor rain event, storm totals are 7 to 11 inches along the
axis, including about 10 inches at Atlanta Hartsfield-Jackson.
Three-hour FFG is below 1 inch across much of the metro and below 1.5
inches over most of north Georgia.

The predecessor rain event will continue into this afternoon before
Hurricane Delphine arrives tonight. Delphine is forecast to make
landfall on the Florida Panhandle this evening and race north along
the Georgia-Alabama line at 45 to 55 mph, passing west of Atlanta
around 2 AM EDT Saturday. Its forward speed will limit totals at any
one point to 3 to 6 inches. However, rain rates of 1 to 2.5 inches
per hour in the core and right-front quadrant will exceed guidance
nearly everywhere on this saturated ground. Considerable to
catastrophic flash flooding is expected, including life-threatening
urban flooding in the Atlanta metro overnight.

A Moderate Risk surrounds the High Risk into the southern
Appalachians, where upslope flow late tonight will enhance rainfall
on already wet slopes. The Slight Risk reaches the Florida Panhandle
coast near landfall, where FFG is higher.
