WATCHES AND WARNINGS

CHANGES WITH THIS ADVISORY:

All Hurricane and Tropical Storm Warnings have been discontinued.

Wind Advisories and High Wind Warnings are in effect for portions of
the Ohio Valley and central Appalachians, and Flood Watches and Flash
Flood Warnings remain in effect across much of Georgia, the western
Carolinas, eastern Tennessee, and Kentucky. For details, please
monitor products issued by your local National Weather Service
forecast office.


DISCUSSION AND OUTLOOK
----------------------
At 500 PM EDT (2100 UTC), the center of Post-Tropical Cyclone
Delphine was located near latitude 38.5 North, longitude 84.5 West.
Delphine is moving toward the north near 14 mph (22 km/h). A turn
toward the north-northeast with a gradual decrease in forward speed
is expected tonight and Sunday as Delphine merges with the parent
trough. On the forecast track, the center will move across eastern
Kentucky and southern Ohio tonight and reach the vicinity of Lake
Erie Sunday afternoon, where it is expected to be absorbed by a
larger extratropical low.

Maximum sustained winds are near 50 mph (85 km/h) with higher gusts.
Gradual weakening is expected, but strong winds will continue well to
the east of the center through Sunday.

Tropical-storm-force winds extend outward up to 275 miles (445 km).

The estimated minimum central pressure is 983 mb (29.03 inches).


HAZARDS AFFECTING LAND
----------------------
RAINFALL:  Delphine is expected to produce an additional 1 to 3
inches of rain, with isolated totals near 5 inches, across eastern
Kentucky, southern Ohio, and western West Virginia through Sunday.
Considerable flash flooding is possible in steep terrain.
Catastrophic flash flooding and major to record river flooding are
ongoing across north Georgia, where storm totals including the
predecessor rain event have reached 10 to 20 inches. Rivers including
the Chattahoochee, Etowah, and Oostanaula will continue rising into
early next week.

WIND:  Wind gusts of 45 to 60 mph are expected tonight across eastern
Kentucky, southern Ohio, and western West Virginia, especially on
ridgetops. Widespread wind damage and power outages from last night's
hurricane-force winds are being reported across Georgia, including
the Atlanta metropolitan area.

TORNADOES:  A couple of tornadoes are possible this evening across
eastern Kentucky and western West Virginia.
