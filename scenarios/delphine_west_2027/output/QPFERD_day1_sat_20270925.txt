...THERE IS A MODERATE RISK OF EXCESSIVE RAINFALL ACROSS PORTIONS OF
MIDDLE AND EAST TENNESSEE...NORTH GEORGIA...AND CENTRAL KENTUCKY...

...Tennessee and Ohio Valleys and the Southern Appalachians...

The High Risk from the previous period was downgraded as Delphine
races into the Tennessee Valley and becomes post-tropical this
morning. A Moderate Risk remains over middle and east Tennessee, far
north Georgia, and central Kentucky, with a broad Slight and Marginal
Risk extending into southern Ohio. Catastrophic flash flooding
occurred overnight across the Atlanta metro and north Georgia, and
major river flooding will continue there for days. River flooding is
not part of this outlook, but those areas remain extremely sensitive
to any additional rain.

As Delphine merges with the parent trough, its forward motion slows
and the rain shield shifts to the northwest of the center, typical of
extratropical transition. A 6 to 10 hour period of moderate to heavy
rain, with rates of 0.5 to 1 inch per hour, is expected across the
Cumberland Plateau and into central Kentucky, bringing totals of 2 to
4 inches with isolated 5 to 6 inches. FFG over Tennessee and Kentucky
is higher than in Georgia, but steep terrain and the fast onset
support a Moderate Risk. Flash flooding in the north Georgia
mountains and near Chattanooga is especially likely where the
predecessor rain event and Delphine's core overlapped.
