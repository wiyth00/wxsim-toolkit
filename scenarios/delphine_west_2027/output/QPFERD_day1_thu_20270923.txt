...THERE IS A MODERATE RISK OF EXCESSIVE RAINFALL ACROSS PORTIONS OF
NORTH GEORGIA...INCLUDING THE ATLANTA METROPOLITAN AREA...AND THE
SOUTH CAROLINA UPSTATE...

...North Georgia and the Southern Appalachians...

The Moderate Risk carried from the Day 2 outlook was maintained and
extended slightly west to the Alabama state line. The Slight and
Marginal Risks were largely unchanged. Observed rainfall over the
past 24 hours was 2 to 4.5 inches along the front from west-central
Georgia through the Atlanta metro to the Upstate, including 3.6
inches at Atlanta Hartsfield-Jackson International Airport. Several
Flash Flood Warnings were issued overnight for urban flooding in the
metro.

The front has not moved and will not move much today, with the
predecessor rain event continuing to be fed by a deep plume of
tropical moisture. PWs of 2.2 to 2.5 inches are near the daily
maximum for late September. Another 3 to 5 inches with localized
amounts over 6 inches are likely along the axis through tonight. The
00Z RRFS and HREF members consistently place the heaviest band from
Carrollton through Atlanta and Gainesville toward Greenville, with
good agreement on placement.

Flash flood guidance has already dropped to 1.5 to 2 inches in 3
hours across the metro. Additional heavy rain on saturated ground
will lead to numerous instances of flash flooding, and some could be
significant, especially in the urban core and along small creeks.
Looking ahead, Hurricane Delphine is forecast to cross Georgia Friday
night. With the ground this wet, a High Risk upgrade is being
considered for the Day 2 outlook.
