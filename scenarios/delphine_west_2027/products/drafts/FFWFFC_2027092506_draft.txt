* At 205 AM EDT, emergency management, fire and rescue crews, and law enforcement reported catastrophic flash flooding across the Atlanta metropolitan area. Doppler radar indicated rainfall rates of 2 to 3 inches per hour in the eyewall and rain bands of Hurricane Delphine, whose center was located near Carrollton, moving north at 45 mph. Two to four inches of rain have fallen in the past two hours on ground saturated by 10 to 12 inches of rain since Wednesday. Additional rainfall of 1 to 2 inches is expected over the next two hours. Flash flooding is ongoing.

  HAZARD...Life-threatening flash flooding. Heavy rain from Hurricane Delphine.

  SOURCE...Emergency management, law enforcement, and fire and rescue reports.

  IMPACT...This is a PARTICULARLY DANGEROUS SITUATION. SEEK HIGHER GROUND NOW! Water rescues are ongoing along Peachtree Creek, Nancy Creek, Proctor Creek, and Sope Creek. Multiple homes and apartments along these creeks are flooded, with water reported to the second floor in some locations. Portions of Interstate 75, Interstate 85, Interstate 285, and Interstate 20 are flooded and impassable. Numerous roads are also blocked by fallen trees and downed power lines. Rapid rises of 10 feet or more are occurring on small streams in less than an hour.

  Atlanta, Sandy Springs, Marietta, Smyrna, Mableton, Decatur, Brookhaven, Dunwoody, Chamblee, Tucker, East Point, College Park, Forest Park, Morrow, Jonesboro, Austell, Douglasville, Lithia Springs, Stone Mountain, Lilburn, Norcross, Conyers, McDonough, Stockbridge, and Fayetteville.

  Interstate 85 between mile markers 66 and 110.
  Interstate 285 in its entirety.

PRECAUTIONARY/PREPAREDNESS ACTIONS...

This is a FLASH FLOOD EMERGENCY for metro Atlanta. This is a PARTICULARLY DANGEROUS SITUATION. SEEK HIGHER GROUND NOW!

Move immediately to the highest floor of a sturdy building if you are in a low-lying area or near a creek. Do not go into an attic unless you have a way to escape through the roof. Do not attempt to travel unless you are fleeing an area subject to flooding or under an evacuation order. Turn Around, Don't Drown when encountering flooded roads. Most flood deaths occur in vehicles.
