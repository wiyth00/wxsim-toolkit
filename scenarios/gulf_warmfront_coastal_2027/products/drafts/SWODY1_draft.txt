Day 1 Convective Outlook
NWS Storm Prediction Center Norman OK
0644 AM CST Wed Feb 24 2027

Valid 241300Z - 251200Z

...THERE IS A HIGH RISK OF SEVERE THUNDERSTORMS ALONG THE MIDDLE AND UPPER TEXAS COAST AND THE COASTAL PARISHES OF SOUTHWEST AND SOUTH-CENTRAL LOUISIANA TO THE MISSISSIPPI RIVER DELTA...

...SUMMARY...
A tornado outbreak is expected today into tonight within a narrow coastal corridor stretching roughly 400 miles from Matagorda Bay through Galveston, Cameron, Vermilion Bay, and Houma to the mouth of the Mississippi River. Numerous tornadoes, several of which will be long-tracked and strong to intense (EF3+), are expected. An EF4+ tornado is possible from Galveston Bay to Vermilion Bay. Supercells moving onshore from the Gulf will travel east-northeast parallel to the coast along a warm front pinned just inland. The threat will continue overnight across coastal southeast Louisiana.

...SYNOPSIS...
A vigorous, compact shortwave trough over northern Coahuila early this morning will eject east-northeast and take on a negative tilt as it crosses the northwest Gulf today. It is embedded within an exceptionally strong subtropical jet, with 250 mb winds of 150-170 kt from northern Mexico into the central Gulf Coast. At the surface, a 1001 mb low just offshore of Port O'Connor will deepen to around 990 mb as it tracks east-northeast. The low will stay within 20-40 miles of the coastline, passing just south of Cameron by 00z and near Grand Isle by 06z. A warm front extends east from the low. It is moving onto the coast this morning but will make little further inland progress, remaining 10-40 miles inland. Its movement will be blocked by a deep, rain-cooled layer across southeast Texas and southern Louisiana, where temperatures are in the 50s F and widespread elevated convection continues. The warm sector is therefore confined to a thin coastal strip and the adjacent Gulf waters.

...Middle/upper Texas coast through coastal Louisiana...
Buoy and platform observations south of the front show dewpoints of 70-74 F over the northwest Gulf. Surface-based MLCAPE of 1500-2500 J/kg is available over the shelf waters and the immediate coastal plain. It falls off sharply inland, to less than 500 J/kg across the Houston, Beaumont, Lake Charles, and Lafayette metros if the front holds as expected. A 70-85 kt low-level jet will surge across the coast from late morning onward, beneath 80-100 kt of effective bulk shear. Surface winds are backed to east-southeast along the front, which will lengthen the low-level hodographs substantially. RRFS and REFS forecast soundings show 0-1 km SRH of 400-600 m2/s2 in the coastal warm sector and 600-800 m2/s2 along the front. Combined with LCLs of 300-600 m, this supports effective-layer STP of 6-12, with some values above 15 along the coast from Galveston Bay to Vermilion Bay this afternoon.

Supercells forming over the Gulf will move east-northeast at 45-55 kt, nearly parallel to the coastline. This is the primary concern. Storms that come ashore will not quickly reach stable air. Instead they can remain in the coastal warm sector and near the front for 100 miles or more, which favors long-track, strong to intense tornadoes. The greatest concern is from the Bolivar Peninsula and the southern Houston/Galveston Bay area east through Sabine Pass, Cameron, and Vermilion Bay from 16z through 01z. The same threat will then shift into Terrebonne, Lafourche, and Plaquemines Parishes from 01z to 07z. Many of these areas are sparsely populated marsh and coastal communities, with limited road networks and evacuation routes. Several tornadoes may occur after dark.

The inland edge of the risk depends heavily on where the warm front stalls. A 15-30 mile northward adjustment would bring the High Risk tornado threat into Houston, Beaumont, Lake Charles, and Lafayette, and trends will be monitored closely through the morning. North of the front, storms will be elevated. They will mainly produce hail and gusty winds, though a tornado or two is possible with any storm that roots along the boundary.

...Southeast Louisiana and the Mississippi/Alabama coast tonight...
The Pacific front will overtake the coastal supercells overnight, and activity will consolidate into a QLCS that sweeps across the New Orleans metro and the Mississippi coast between 06z and 10z. MLCAPE of 500-1000 J/kg and 400+ m2/s2 of 0-1 km SRH will persist near the coast. The line will produce mesovortex tornadoes and 60-80 mph gusts before the severe threat decreases near Mobile Bay toward 12z.

...Inland southeast Texas into central Louisiana and southern Mississippi...
Elevated storms atop the rain-cooled layer may produce isolated large hail of 1-1.75 inches and occasional damaging gusts through tonight.
