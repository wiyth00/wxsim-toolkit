...THERE IS A HIGH RISK OF SEVERE THUNDERSTORMS ACROSS EAST-CENTRAL
IOWA...NORTHERN ILLINOIS...NORTHERN INDIANA...AND EXTREME NORTHWEST
OHIO...

...SUMMARY...
A particularly dangerous and widespread severe wind event and high-end
derecho will develop early this afternoon across eastern Iowa and
race east-southeastward across northern Illinois, the Chicago
metropolitan area, northern Indiana, and northwest Ohio through this
evening. Widespread destructive straight-line winds with swaths of
80 to 105 mph gusts will produce significant, extensive structural
and tree damage and widespread, prolonged power outages. Several
embedded tornadoes, a few strong, and isolated large hail are also
expected.

...Corn Belt into the Southern Great Lakes / Ohio Valley...
...Meteorological Analysis...
A classic, high-end progressive derecho environment is established
across the Upper Mississippi Valley and southern Great Lakes today.
A fast quasi-zonal midlevel jet streak (60-70 kt at 500 mb) is
progressing across the northern flank of an intense mid-summer
subtropical ridge centered over the Ozarks and lower Mississippi
Valley. At the surface, a stationary to slow-moving warm frontal
boundary and reinforced antecedent convective outflow boundary is
draped from central Iowa through northern Illinois (near the I-88 and
I-80 corridors) into northern Indiana and northern Ohio.

South of this boundary, boundary-layer conditions are extreme.
Strong solar insolation and intense agricultural evapotranspiration
have pushed surface temperatures into the lower 90s F amidst
widespread dewpoints of 77 to 81 F. This is generating extreme
thermodynamic instability, with MLCAPE values of 4500 to 5500 J/kg
and locally near 6000 J/kg where moisture pooling is maximized.
Sampled midlevel lapse rates are steep (7.8 to 8.4 C/km) within an
advecting EML plume. Precipitable water values of 2.0 to 2.2 inches,
combined with relatively dry air near 700-600 mb, yield exceptional
downdraft CAPE (DCAPE) values of 1500 to 1800 J/kg, creating an ideal
thermodynamic setup for severe downburst generation and cold-pool
acceleration.

Kinematically, 0-6 km bulk shear of 50 to 60 kt oriented parallel to
the east-southeastward oriented boundary will favor very rapid upscale
growth of early discrete to semi-discrete storms into an organized,
forward-propagating mesoscale convective system (MCS). As the system
matures, intense rear-inflow jet (RIJ) descent into the forward flank
will drive destructive bowing segments and apex surges, with the
derecho expected to forward-propagate at 55 to 65 kt.

...Evolution & Timing...
Intense convective initiation is anticipated between 18z and 20z
along the boundary in east-central Iowa, near and east of Waterloo and
Des Moines, within an uncapping, highly unstable air mass. While
initial cells may produce isolated large to very large hail (up to 2
inches) and a couple of discrete tornadoes, upscale growth into a
linear complex is expected to occur rapidly within 60 to 90 minutes of
initiation.

Once a consolidated cold pool forms, the system will surge
east-southeastward across the Quad Cities, Rockford, and the greater
Chicago metropolitan area between 21z and 01z, before crossing northern
Indiana (South Bend, Fort Wayne) and reaching northwest Ohio (Toledo,
Findlay, Cleveland vicinity) between 01z and 04z. 

Extensive swaths of 75 to 90 mph winds are expected along the path of
the bow echo, with peak corridor winds of 95 to 105+ mph where
mesoscale rear-inflow channeling and severe downbursts couple with the
fast ambient steering flow. The density of population and infrastructure
in the path of this system warrants the issuance of a High Risk for
destructive winds. In addition, intense mesovortices along the leading
edge of the bowing line and within the northern bookend vortex will be
capable of producing several tornadoes (a few potentially EF2+).

The severe wind and QLCS tornado threat will gradually taper late
tonight toward Lake Erie and central Ohio as the convective system
outruns the greatest instability reservoir, though damaging winds may
continue as far east as western Pennsylvania before dawn.
