...THERE IS A HIGH RISK OF SEVERE THUNDERSTORMS ACROSS THE UPPER
TEXAS COAST INTO SOUTHWEST AND SOUTH-CENTRAL LOUISIANA...

...SUMMARY...
A regional tornado outbreak is expected from late morning through
this evening across the Houston/Galveston area eastward through
Beaumont, Lake Charles, Lafayette, and the lower Atchafalaya basin
into the Houma area. Several strong to intense tornadoes are likely,
and a few long-track, potentially violent tornadoes are possible.
The greatest threat will be concentrated along a narrow coastal
warm-sector corridor. Tornadoes and damaging winds will continue
farther east into southeast Louisiana tonight.

...SYNOPSIS...
A negatively tilted shortwave trough will eject from northeastern
Mexico into east Texas and the lower Mississippi Valley today. An
80-100 kt midlevel jet will overspread the northwest Gulf Coast,
while a surface low deepens over southeast Texas and moves
northeastward into central Louisiana this evening. A warm front
extending east from the low will move inland from the Gulf this
morning and become nearly stationary near the Interstate 10
corridor. Persistent precipitation to its north will maintain a
cool, stable air mass over much of interior east Texas and central
Louisiana. A trailing cold front will progress east across the
coastal warm sector late today and tonight.

...Upper Texas coast into southwest Louisiana...
Rich maritime moisture will spread inland with the warm front,
yielding surface dewpoints of 68-72 F. Despite cloud cover, modest
warming and cooling aloft will support MLCAPE of 1500-2500 J/kg.
A 55-70 kt southerly low-level jet above backed east-southeasterly
surface winds will produce strongly curved low-level hodographs.
Effective bulk shear of 60-80 kt, 0-1 km storm-relative helicity of
350-550 m2/s2, and cloud bases generally below 800 m will favor
sustained tornadic supercells where inflow remains surface based.

Late-morning storms moving inland from the Gulf and additional
warm-sector development ahead of the cold front will provide
multiple opportunities for tornadic supercells. The most favorable
period across Houston/Galveston and Beaumont is approximately
16-22Z, with the threat increasing across southwest Louisiana
around 19-01Z. Discrete and semi-discrete storm modes are expected
initially, with sufficient spacing for several sustained supercells.

The High Risk is deliberately confined to the coastal plain.
Storms close to the warm front will ingest very moist air with
enhanced low-level shear, while storms crossing appreciably north
of it will encounter stable near-surface air. East-northeastward
storm motion and an east-northeast-oriented segment of the front
may allow some supercells to remain close to the boundary for
extended periods. Repeated development along warm-sector
confluence bands will also concentrate the threat in this corridor.
Several strong to intense tornadoes are expected; localized
conditions support the possibility of violent tornadoes.

...South-central and southeast Louisiana...
The favorable surface-based corridor will extend east through
Lafayette, New Iberia, Morgan City, and Houma during the afternoon
and evening. MLCAPE of 1000-2000 J/kg and an intensifying low-level
jet will sustain the tornado threat after sunset. The greatest
concern is for supercells remaining discrete ahead of the cold
front, particularly near the warm front and its intersections with
residual outflow boundaries. The principal threat window is
22-05Z, with some overlap between successive regional windows.

Increasing frontal forcing and storm interactions should promote
upscale growth during the evening. A broken line with embedded
supercells will spread toward Baton Rouge, New Orleans, and the
Mississippi coast overnight. Strong tornadoes remain possible
with persistent embedded supercells or discrete storms ahead of
the line, along with locally 65-80 mph gusts. The broader Moderate
Risk covers this later threat, while the High Risk follows the
more favorable coastal supercell corridor west and southwest of
the New Orleans metro. Large hail of 1-1.75 inches is possible,
but tornadoes are the primary driver of the High Risk.

...Forecast confidence and northern extent...
High confidence in surface-based storm development and substantial
supercell coverage within the narrow warm sector supports the
High Risk. The largest remaining uncertainty is the inland reach
of the warm front. A displacement of only a few tens of miles
would shift the corridor of greatest tornado potential. Widespread
cold-pool production could shorten the discrete-supercell phase;
conversely, a slower cold front would prolong it. Areas north of
the boundary will primarily experience elevated thunderstorms,
with a lower tornado threat unless surface-based instability
spreads farther inland than forecast.
