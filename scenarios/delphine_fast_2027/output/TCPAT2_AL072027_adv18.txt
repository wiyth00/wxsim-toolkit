DISCUSSION AND OUTLOOK
----------------------
At 500 PM EDT (2100 UTC), the center of Hurricane Delphine was
located near latitude 27.1 North, longitude 85.3 West. Delphine is
moving toward the north-northeast near 25 mph (41 km/h). Delphine is
expected to accelerate further tonight as it is captured by a strong
mid-level trough over the lower Mississippi Valley, with forward
speeds of 40 to 50 mph over Georgia. On the forecast track, the
center of Delphine will reach the Florida Big Bend coast between 10
PM and midnight EDT, then race north-northeastward across Georgia
overnight, passing near or just east of the Atlanta metropolitan area
around 2 AM EDT Saturday. Delphine is expected to turn north and slow
down as it merges with the trough over the Ohio Valley Saturday and
Sunday.

Maximum sustained winds are near 120 mph (195 km/h) with higher
gusts. Delphine is a major hurricane -- a category 3 on the
Saffir-Simpson Hurricane Wind Scale. Little change in strength is
expected before landfall. Because of Delphine's extremely fast
forward speed, only gradual weakening is expected as it moves across
Georgia tonight, and it is forecast to still be a hurricane when it
reaches the Atlanta area. Delphine is forecast to become a powerful
post-tropical cyclone over the southern Appalachians Saturday morning
as it merges with the trough. Its wind field will expand, and
damaging winds will extend far from the center through the weekend.

Hurricane-force winds extend outward up to 80 miles (130 km) from the
center and tropical-storm-force winds extend outward up to 255 miles
(405 km).

The estimated minimum central pressure is 955 mb (28.20 inches).


HAZARDS AFFECTING LAND
----------------------
STORM SURGE:  The combination of a dangerous storm surge and the tide
will cause normally dry areas near the coast to be flooded by rising
waters moving inland from the shoreline. Because of Delphine's fast
motion, the water will rise very rapidly, in some places by several
feet in less than an hour. The water could reach the following
heights above ground somewhere in the indicated areas if the peak
surge occurs at the time of high tide...
  - Carrabelle to Suwannee River, FL...15-22 ft
  - Suwannee River to Yankeetown, FL...12-16 ft
  - Ochlockonee River to Carrabelle, FL...8-12 ft
  - Yankeetown to Anclote River, FL...6-10 ft
  - Anclote River to Middle of Longboat Key, FL, including Tampa
    Bay...4-7 ft
  - Indian Pass to Carrabelle, FL...3-5 ft

WIND:  Hurricane conditions are expected within the Hurricane Warning
area along the coast this evening. Destructive hurricane-force winds
will spread well inland across north Florida and much of Georgia
overnight, including the Atlanta, Macon, Athens, and Augusta
metropolitan areas, and into the Upstate of South Carolina by early
Saturday morning. The strongest winds, with gusts of 100 to 120 mph,
are expected on the east side of the track. After two days of heavy
rain, soils are saturated across north Georgia, so widespread tree
falls, structural damage, and power outages lasting days to weeks are
expected. Many of these winds will occur in the middle of the night.
Residents should be in their safe place, away from trees and windows,
before winds increase this evening.

RAINFALL:  A predecessor rain event has already produced 6 to 12
inches of rain across north Georgia since Wednesday, including about
10 inches at Atlanta Hartsfield-Jackson International Airport.
Delphine will produce an additional 3 to 6 inches of rain, with
isolated totals near 8 inches, across Georgia, the western Carolinas,
and the southern Appalachians through Saturday. On top of saturated
ground and running-high streams, this will cause considerable to
catastrophic flash and urban flooding and significant to major river
flooding. Landslides are possible in the southern Appalachians.

TORNADOES:  Several tornadoes are possible tonight and early Saturday
across the eastern Florida Panhandle, north Florida, Georgia, and
South Carolina. These tornadoes will be moving very quickly and will
be wrapped in rain, in many cases overnight.

SURF:  Swells generated by Delphine are affecting much of the Gulf
Coast and the west coast of Florida. These swells are likely to cause
life-threatening surf and rip current conditions.
