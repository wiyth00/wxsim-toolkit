Day 1 Convective Outlook
NWS Storm Prediction Center Norman OK
0644 AM CST Wed Feb 24 2027

Valid 241300Z - 251200Z

...THERE IS A HIGH RISK OF SEVERE THUNDERSTORMS FROM THE UPPER TEXAS COAST AND GREATER HOUSTON AREA EAST ACROSS SOUTHWEST INTO SOUTH-CENTRAL LOUISIANA...

...SUMMARY...
A regional tornado outbreak is expected today into tonight along a narrow corridor from the Houston metro and Galveston Bay east through Beaumont, Lake Charles, and Lafayette. Several long-track, strong to intense tornadoes (EF3+) are likely, and an EF4+ tornado is possible. Supercells will track east-northeast along a stalled coastal warm front near Interstate 10 from late morning through the evening. Tornadoes and damaging winds will continue overnight into southeast Louisiana, including the Baton Rouge and New Orleans areas.

...SYNOPSIS...
A vigorous, compact shortwave trough over northern Coahuila early this morning will eject east-northeast and take on a negative tilt as it crosses south Texas and the northwest Gulf today. It is embedded within an exceptionally strong subtropical jet, with 250 mb winds of 150-170 kt from northern Mexico into the lower Mississippi Valley, a pattern typical of this strong El Nino winter. At the surface, a 1001 mb low near Matagorda Bay will deepen to around 992 mb as it tracks east-northeast along the coast, reaching the Sabine Pass vicinity by 00z and south-central Mississippi by 12z. A warm front extends east from the low, 20-40 miles offshore of the upper Texas and southwest Louisiana coast. It will move inland this morning, then stall near or just north of I-10 from the northern Houston suburbs to near Lafayette. North of the front, persistent rain and elevated convection have left a deep, rain-cooled layer across east Texas and central Louisiana, with temperatures in the 50s F. A Pacific cold front trailing the low will sweep east across the Texas coastal plain this afternoon and across Louisiana tonight.

...Upper Texas coast into southwest and south-central Louisiana...
The environment along and south of the warm front will support significant tornadoes. Buoy and platform observations over the northwest Gulf show dewpoints of 70-73 F. This modified Gulf air will spread onshore behind the warm front, and dewpoints will reach the upper 60s to near 70 F along the coastal plain by late morning. Temperatures in the mid 70s F and midlevel lapse rates of 6-6.5 C/km will yield MLCAPE of 1200-2000 J/kg near the coast. MLCAPE will fall sharply to less than 500 J/kg within 30-50 miles north of the front. A 65-80 kt south-southwesterly low-level jet will overspread the region from late morning onward, beneath 80-100 kt of effective bulk shear. Surface winds are backed to east-southeast along the boundary, which will greatly enlarge the low-level hodographs. RRFS and REFS forecast soundings show 0-1 km SRH of 350-500 m2/s2 in the open warm sector and 500-700 m2/s2 along the front itself. Combined with LCLs of 400-700 m, this supports effective-layer STP of 5-10, with local values above 12 along the boundary between Houston and Lafayette this afternoon.

Warm-advection storms over the Gulf this morning should move onshore across Galveston Bay and the Bolivar Peninsula by 15-17z. They will quickly become surface-based and acquire supercell characteristics as they reach the warm front. Additional discrete supercells are expected from 17z through 00z, developing west and southwest of Houston ahead of the Pacific front and along confluence bands in the warm sector. Storm motions of 240-250 degrees at 40-50 kt are nearly parallel to the warm front. Supercells will therefore be able to ride the boundary for long distances instead of crossing into the stable air to the north. This is the primary concern for long-track, strong to intense tornadoes from the Houston metro through Beaumont, Lake Charles, and Lafayette. The corridor is narrow, and its northern edge is tied closely to the warm front's position. Where it stalls will be critical, and a northward adjustment of 15-30 miles remains possible later this morning. North of the front, storms will quickly become elevated and pose mainly a hail and isolated wind threat. Tornado potential will persist after dark as the low-level jet strengthens further. Several tornadic supercells may remain ongoing across south-central Louisiana (Lafayette to Morgan City and Houma) through 03-05z.

...Southeast Louisiana and coastal Mississippi tonight...
The Pacific front will overtake the warm-sector supercells this evening, and activity will consolidate into a strong QLCS across south-central Louisiana by 03z. The line will then sweep east across the Baton Rouge and New Orleans metros and the Mississippi coast between 05z and 10z. Instability will weaken somewhat overnight, but surface-based MLCAPE of 500-1000 J/kg and 400+ m2/s2 of 0-1 km SRH will remain. This will support line-embedded mesovortex tornadoes, a few of which could be strong, along with 60-80 mph gusts where bowing segments develop. Any discrete cells ahead of the line near the coast would pose a greater tornado threat. The severe threat should diminish toward 12z as the line reaches southwest Alabama and the instability becomes increasingly marginal.

...Southeast and east Texas into central Louisiana and southern Mississippi...
Farther north, along the periphery of the warm sector, isolated large hail of 1-1.75 inches and damaging gusts are possible with elevated storms atop the rain-cooled layer. Surface-based storms and a greater tornado risk will extend this far north only if the warm front advances farther inland than forecast.
