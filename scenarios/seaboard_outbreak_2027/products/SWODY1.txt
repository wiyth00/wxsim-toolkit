NWS Storm Prediction Center Norman OK
0744 AM CDT Thu Apr 29 2027

Valid 291300Z - 301200Z

...THERE IS A MODERATE RISK OF SEVERE THUNDERSTORMS FROM SOUTHEAST
GEORGIA ACROSS MUCH OF THE CAROLINAS AND VIRGINIA INTO
MARYLAND...DELAWARE...AND SOUTHEAST PENNSYLVANIA...

...SUMMARY...
A widespread and dangerous severe weather outbreak is expected today
from southeast Georgia and the Carolinas north through Virginia and
the Delmarva into southeast Pennsylvania. Numerous tornadoes, some
strong to intense (EF3+), are possible with discrete supercells from
the coastal plain of the Carolinas into southeast Virginia and the
Delmarva. A fast-moving, intense squall line will also sweep east
across the region from midday into the evening. It will produce
widespread damaging winds, with gusts of 85-100 mph possible along
the I-95 corridor from central North Carolina to Philadelphia, along
with embedded tornadoes.

...SYNOPSIS...
A deep, negatively tilted upper trough over the Ohio Valley this
morning will pivot rapidly northeast through the period. A 110-130 kt
500 mb jet streak rounding its base will overspread the Carolinas and
Virginia by early afternoon, then the Mid-Atlantic by evening. At the
surface, a 984 mb low over central Pennsylvania will deepen to around
976 mb as it moves into northern New York by 00z. A warm front now
draped from near Richmond to the southern Delmarva will surge north
to near Philadelphia by midafternoon. The trailing cold front will
cross the Appalachians today and reach the coast late tonight. A
strong pre-frontal trough is already evident across the western
Piedmont.

...Carolinas into Virginia and the Delmarva...
An exceptionally moist warm sector is in place for late April.
Surface dewpoints are in the upper 60s to low 70s F across the
Carolinas and southeast Virginia, and mid 60s F are already streaming
north into the Delmarva. Combined with modest midlevel lapse rates
and some morning sun, this will yield MLCAPE of 1500-2500 J/kg across
the Carolinas and 1000-1500 J/kg into Virginia and Maryland. The wind
field is extreme. Effective bulk shear of 70-90 kt and a 60-75 kt
southerly low-level jet will produce large, looping low-level
hodographs, with 0-1 km SRH of 300-500 m2/s2 and effective-layer STP
of 4-8 in both the RRFS and REFS.

Scattered discrete supercells should develop within the warm sector
by 16-17z, ahead of the main line, along confluence bands over the
coastal plain of South Carolina and North Carolina. Given the
moisture, low LCLs (500-800 m), and very strong low-level shear,
these storms will be capable of long-track, strong to intense
tornadoes as they race north-northeast at 50-60 kt. The greatest
concentration of intense tornado potential is from near Florence and
Fayetteville through Raleigh and Rocky Mount into southeast Virginia
(Richmond to Hampton Roads) and the lower Delmarva, mainly 17z-00z.

...Squall line: Piedmont to the I-95 corridor...
A squall line now over the Blue Ridge and western Piedmont will
accelerate east this afternoon as the cold pool strengthens beneath
the intense wind field. It is expected to reach the I-95 corridor
from Fayetteville to Richmond around 20-22z and Washington,
Baltimore, and Philadelphia between 22z and 01z. Line-embedded
mesovortices and QLCS tornadoes are likely, and 85-100 mph gusts are
possible where bowing segments develop, which supports the Intensity
2 wind area. Wind damage is likely to be widespread in the densely
populated corridor from Richmond to Philadelphia.

...Southeast Georgia and southern South Carolina...
Midlevel lapse rates are somewhat steeper farther south, and this
area remains farther from the strongest forcing. Discrete to
semi-discrete supercells are still expected this afternoon, with
tornadoes, damaging winds, and large hail, a few stones up to 2
inches in diameter. The threat should diminish after 03z as the line
moves offshore.
