0902 AM CDT Thu Apr 29 2027

Areas affected...Eastern North Carolina...northeast South

Concerning...Severe potential...PDS Tornado Watch likely

Valid 291402Z - 291600Z

Probability of Watch Issuance...95 percent

SUMMARY...A PDS Tornado Watch will likely be issued within the hour.
Discrete supercells are beginning to develop in an exceptionally
moist and strongly sheared warm sector, and several long-track,
intense tornadoes (EF3+) are possible from the Pee Dee region of
South Carolina through eastern North Carolina into southeast
Virginia. SPC will issue a special Day 1 Outlook update at 1430Z,
which will include a High Risk for this area, driven by tornadoes.

DISCUSSION...Visible satellite imagery shows the morning stratus deck
across the coastal plain breaking up rapidly. Agitated cumulus bands
are organizing along several confluence axes from near Florence and
Lumberton to Goldsboro and Rocky Mount. The first deep towers are now
evident near Lumberton and west of Goldsboro. Surface dewpoints have
risen to 69-71 F as far north as Greenville and Rocky Mount, and to
65-67 F in southeast Virginia. Temperatures are already in the mid
70s F beneath the breaks. Modified 12z MHX and special 13z RAH
soundings yield MLCAPE of 1500-2200 J/kg with little remaining
inhibition, and mixed-layer LCLs are only 500-800 m.

The kinematic environment is extreme. KRAX, KMHX, and KAKQ VWPs show
60-70 kt of south-southwest flow at 1 km, beneath 90+ kt at 3 km. 0-1
km SRH is 350-500 m2/s2 and effective bulk shear is 75-85 kt.
Effective STP values of 5-9 already cover much of eastern North
Carolina and should expand north toward the southeast Virginia warm
front by midday. Storm motions near 220/55 kt will carry any
supercell a long way within this environment. Once storms mature, the
combination of low LCLs, rich moisture, and very large low-level
hodographs strongly favors tornadic supercells, including the
potential for long-track, violent tornadoes.

The main uncertainty is storm coverage and mode. Numerous storms
could evolve into clusters, but current trends and
convection-allowing guidance support several discrete to
semi-discrete supercells well ahead of the squall line now crossing
the western Piedmont. That line will reach the I-95 corridor later
this afternoon (see MCD 0612 for areas north). Given the magnitude
and duration of the threat, a PDS Tornado Watch is preferred.
