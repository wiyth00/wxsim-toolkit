Mesoscale Discussion 0612
NWS Storm Prediction Center Norman OK
0909 AM CDT Thu Apr 29 2027

Areas affected...Central and northern Virginia...the District of
Columbia...Maryland...Delaware...southeast Pennsylvania...and
southwest New Jersey

Concerning...Severe potential...PDS Severe Thunderstorm Watch likely

Valid 291409Z - 291700Z

Probability of Watch Issuance...95 percent

SUMMARY...An intense, fast-moving squall line will race
east-northeast across the region from late morning through the
afternoon. Widespread damaging winds are expected, including embedded
gusts of 85-100 mph, along with a few brief QLCS tornadoes near the
warm front. A PDS Severe Thunderstorm Watch will likely be issued by
16z. SPC will issue a special Day 1 Outlook update at 1430Z, which
will include a High Risk for this corridor, driven by damaging winds.

DISCUSSION...The squall line extended at 14z from near Frederick MD
to Charlottesville and Lynchburg, and it is moving east at 45-55 kt.
It has recently become more organized, with a sharpening leading edge
and a developing rear-inflow jet evident in KLWX velocity data. Gusts
of 60-70 mph have been measured at SHD and OKV over the past hour,
and several bowing segments are forming west of Culpeper and near
Front Royal. The pressure-rise/fall couplet along the line is now 6-8
mb in 2 hours, and the cold pool is strengthening.

Ahead of the line, the warm front has lifted through Richmond and the
Northern Neck and is surging north toward the District and southern
Maryland at about 25 kt. It should reach the Baltimore to Dover axis
by 16-17z and near Philadelphia by 18z. Dewpoints are already 60-63 F
north to the Potomac, and upper 60s F are lurking just south. Behind
the warm front, MLCAPE should rise to 800-1500 J/kg. Although that is
modest, it sits beneath an extraordinary wind field. The KLWX and
KDOX VWPs show 70-80 kt at 1-2 km AGL, and 0-3 km bulk shear exceeds
60 kt oriented nearly normal to the line. Line-embedded mesovortices
may produce a few tornadoes, especially along and just south of the
advancing warm front, where 0-1 km SRH is 400-600 m2/s2. The tornado
potential is localized and secondary to the destructive straight-line
wind threat. Momentum transfer from the low-level jet will support a
swath of 85-100 mph gusts, most likely along I-95 from Fredericksburg
through Washington and Baltimore to Wilmington and Philadelphia.

Farther north toward Harrisburg and Allentown, the environment will
remain more stable until the warm front arrives, and the tornado
threat there is more conditional. Very strong gusts are still
possible where the line becomes surface-based.
