...SUMMARY...
An exceptionally dangerous severe-weather outbreak is underway across
the Atlantic Seaboard. Numerous tornadoes, including several
long-track, intense tornadoes (EF3+), are expected across eastern
North Carolina and southeast Virginia this afternoon. Separately, a
fast-moving squall line will produce widespread destructive wind
gusts of 85 to 100 mph from northern Virginia across the
Washington-Baltimore corridor into Delaware and southeast
Pennsylvania. A few brief tornadoes may occur within the line. The
two High Risk areas and their broad surrounding Moderate Risk remain
in place.

...1630Z UPDATE...
This update maintains both High Risk areas and the associated 30

...EASTERN NORTH CAROLINA INTO SOUTHEAST VIRGINIA...
Discrete to semi-discrete supercells are now maturing from the
Fayetteville and Raleigh areas eastward toward Goldsboro and Rocky
Mount. Additional development remains possible farther southwest
across the Pee Dee. Dewpoints of 69 to 72 F and continued heating
support MLCAPE of 1800 to 2500 J/kg beneath effective shear of 75 to
85 kt. Low cloud bases and 0-1 km SRH of 350 to 500 m2/s2 favor rapid
intensification of low-level mesocyclones. Storms moving
north-northeast at 50 to 60 kt can remain in the favorable warm
sector for several hours. Multiple long-track tornadoes, including
intense and possibly violent tornadoes, are expected through late
afternoon. The most concentrated threat now extends from Fayetteville
and Raleigh through Goldsboro, Wilson, Rocky Mount, and Emporia
toward the Suffolk and Hampton Roads areas. Large hail and damaging
winds will accompany the strongest cells.

...NORTHERN VIRGINIA THROUGH SOUTHEAST PENNSYLVANIA...
The organized squall line is advancing into the Washington-Baltimore
corridor at 45 to 55 kt and will continue northeast toward Wilmington
and Philadelphia through midafternoon. A strengthening rear-inflow
jet and 70 to 80 kt of flow at 1 to 2 km AGL support a corridor of
widespread damaging wind, with embedded gusts of 85 to 100 mph
possible. The most intense swath remains likely near I-95 and
adjacent communities, though damaging gusts can extend well beyond
the High Risk area. A few brief QLCS tornadoes may occur,
particularly near and south of the northward-moving warm front, but
destructive straight-line wind is the dominant hazard. The tornado
threat becomes more conditional north of the front toward Harrisburg
and Allentown.

...SOUTHEAST GEORGIA AND SOUTH CAROLINA...
The broad Moderate Risk surrounding the southern High Risk remains
warranted. Additional storms along the pre-frontal trough and
confluence bands may produce damaging winds, large hail, and
tornadoes from coastal Georgia across eastern South Carolina. The
greatest potential for long-track, intense tornadoes remains farther
north and east across eastern North Carolina and southeast Virginia.
Continued monitoring is needed for any more discrete development
ahead of the main line.
