...ICE STORM WARNING REMAINS IN EFFECT UNTIL 7 PM EST FRIDAY...

* WHAT...Catastrophic ice storm. Freezing rain, heavy at times, with
  embedded thundersleet. Additional ice accumulations of one-half to
  one and one-quarter inches. Total ice accumulations of 1 to 2
  inches in a narrow band from Carrollton through Atlanta and
  Lawrenceville to Commerce and Hartwell, locally up to 2 inches near
  Hartwell and Royston. Totals of one-half to 1 inch elsewhere across
  the metro area and west Georgia, and 0.25 to 0.50 inch near
  Covington, Madison, and Washington. Northeast winds 15 to 25 mph
  with gusts up to 45 mph.

* WHERE...Metro Atlanta, northeast Georgia, and west-central Georgia,
  including the I-85 and I-20 corridors.

* WHEN...Until 7 PM EST Friday. The heaviest freezing rain is
  expected through Thursday night. Ice will not melt until Saturday
  afternoon, with temperatures staying below freezing until then.

* IMPACTS...Catastrophic damage from ice is expected and is already
  occurring. Widespread and long-lasting power outages are expected.
  Many trees will snap or topple under the weight of the ice, taking
  down power lines and blocking nearly every road. Outages could last
  one to two weeks or longer in the hardest-hit areas, and some
  utility poles and transmission towers may fail. Travel will be
  impossible. Roads, bridges, and sidewalks are sheet ice.
  Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport will be closed or
  severely limited.

* ADDITIONAL DETAILS...This is the worst ice storm in the modern
  record for metro Atlanta, far beyond the February 2014 event. Ice
  thickness of 1 inch or more adds hundreds of pounds to each tree
  limb, and winds gusting to 45 mph will bring down trees well beyond
  the usual breaking point. The heaviest ice is falling in bands only
  10 to 20 miles wide, so totals can differ by half an inch or more
  over a short distance. Do not assume that a lull in the freezing
  rain means the worst has passed. A thin zone near the southern edge
  of the warning may see more rain and less ice if the cold air
  weakens, but gusts there could reach 60 mph.

PRECAUTIONARY/PREPAREDNESS ACTIONS...

Do not travel. If you must go outside, watch for falling limbs and
downed power lines, and assume that every wire is live. Stay out of
rooms and off lanes under large trees. Plan to be without power for
many days. Run generators outdoors and well away from windows, and
never use grills or camp stoves indoors. Charge phones and have a
battery-powered radio. Check on neighbors who rely on electric
medical equipment, and call 911 only for life-threatening
emergencies.
