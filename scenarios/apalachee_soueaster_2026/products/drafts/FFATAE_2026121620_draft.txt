...FLOOD WATCH IN EFFECT FROM 7 PM EST /6 PM CST/ THIS EVENING THROUGH SATURDAY MORNING...

THIS IS A PARTICULARLY DANGEROUS SITUATION.

* WHAT...Catastrophic flash flooding and major river flooding caused by excessive rainfall. Storm totals of 6 to 10 inches are expected, with locally 12 inches or more near Valdosta, Quitman, and Thomasville. Rain rates of 1 to 2 inches per hour are likely tonight and again Thursday as the low stalls over southwest Georgia.

* WHERE...All of the Florida Big Bend and Panhandle, southwest and south-central Georgia, and southeast Alabama.

* WHEN...From 7 PM EST /6 PM CST/ this evening through Saturday morning.

* IMPACTS...Life-threatening flooding of creeks, streams, urban areas, and low-lying roads, much of it at night. Many homes and businesses will flood, including some that have never flooded before. Major flooding is expected on the Ochlockonee, Withlacoochee, Aucilla, Little, Flint, Alapaha, and Kinchafoonee Rivers, continuing well into next week. Rescues will be extremely difficult with roads blocked by fallen trees and power lines.

* ADDITIONAL DETAILS...
  - This is not a typical flood event. It is happening at the same time as destructive winds of 80 to 125 mph and 10 to 15 feet of coastal flooding in the Big Bend. All three will make one another worse.
  - Floodwaters will be extremely dangerous to touch. Expect energized power lines lying in the water, nails and broken lumber from damaged buildings, large tree limbs moving with the current, fuel and chemicals, and raw sewage. Widespread power outages will knock out sewage lift stations and treatment plants, so sewage overflows are likely in Tallahassee, Valdosta, Albany, and Dothan.
  - Fallen trees and debris will block storm drains, ditches, and culverts, sending water into streets and neighborhoods that normally drain well. High water from the coastal flooding will keep rivers from draining into the Gulf through Thursday.
  - The ground is already saturated after the wettest El Nino autumn on record, so nearly all of this rain will run off.
  - Power outages and damaged cell towers may knock out your ability to receive warnings overnight.

PRECAUTIONARY/PREPAREDNESS ACTIONS...

This is a Particularly Dangerous Situation. You should monitor later forecasts and be alert for Flash Flood Warnings and Flash Flood Emergencies. Have a plan to reach higher ground before the rain arrives this evening, and do not wait for the water to rise.

Never walk, swim, or drive through floodwaters. They may hide downed power lines carrying a deadly current, along with sharp debris and sewage. Assume every downed line is live. Turn around, don't drown.

If your home floods, get to the highest floor, but do not go into a closed attic, where you could be trapped. After the water goes down, avoid contact with it and anything it touched, wash with soap and clean water, and keep children and pets away. Use bottled water until officials say the tap water is safe.
