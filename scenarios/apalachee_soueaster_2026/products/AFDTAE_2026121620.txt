.KEY MESSAGES...

  - A rapidly deepening extratropical low over the eastern Gulf is
    forecast to come ashore near St. Marks around 1 AM EST Thursday
    with a central pressure near 941 mb. That would be the lowest
    pressure on record for a non-tropical low anywhere along the Gulf
    Coast. The low will then loop slowly over southwest Georgia
    through Friday before exiting east Saturday.

  - A High Wind Warning is in effect for the entire area from 7 PM
    EST this evening through 7 PM EST Friday. Gusts of 80 to 110 mph
    are expected along and east of the track, with peak gusts near
    125 mph possible from Perry to Valdosta. Gusts of 75 to 95 mph
    are expected west of the Apalachicola River. Expect widespread,
    long-duration power outages, numerous downed trees, and
    structural damage. If the extreme winds near the core
    materialize, Extreme Wind Warnings will be issued as the low
    comes ashore.

  - A Coastal Flood Warning is in effect for the Big Bend coast.
    Inundation of 10 to 15 feet above ground is forecast from Keaton
    Beach to Steinhatchee, 8 to 12 feet from St. Marks to Econfina,
    and 4 to 7 feet along the Franklin County coast. The water will
    rise very quickly late tonight. Leave evacuation zones before
    dark.

  - A squall line with embedded supercells will cross the Big Bend
    this evening. SPC has an Enhanced Risk (level 3 of 5) for the
    southeast Big Bend. Tornadoes may be brief and rain-wrapped, with
    little warning.

  - WPC has a High Risk (level 4 of 4) of excessive rainfall for
    Thursday and Thursday night across southwest Georgia, the Big
    Bend and southeast Alabama, the first winter High Risk on record
    here. Storm totals of 6 to 10 inches, locally 12 near Valdosta,
    will fall on ground already saturated by the wettest El Nino
    autumn on record. A Particularly Dangerous Situation Flood Watch
    is in effect for the entire area. Catastrophic flash flooding and
    major river flooding are expected, and floodwaters will be full
    of downed power lines, debris, and sewage.

.NEAR TERM...
(Through Thursday)
Issued at 342 PM EST Wed Dec 16 2026

The 18Z surface analysis put the low near 27.9N 87.0W, about 230
miles southwest of Apalachicola, at 962 mb and falling about 3 mb per
hour. That is 35 mb in the last 12 hours, a pace of more than 5
Bergerons once adjusted for latitude, and the bottom is not in yet.
This is not a tropical cyclone and does not have a closed warm core
in the usual sense, but it is acting like one near the center.
GOES-19 shows a clear slot and a tightly wrapped cloud head around a
developing warm seclusion, and recon by an Air Force C-130 out of
Keesler at 1830Z found 87 kt at 1500 ft southeast of the center. The
setup is about as favorable for explosive deepening as the Gulf ever
gets. An extremely strong subtropical jet with 200-plus kt at 250 mb
sits over the north Gulf, and a sharp northern-stream wave is phasing
with it over Louisiana. The Loop Current eddy has kept sea surface
temperatures 2 to 3 C above normal because no hurricane mixed the
Gulf this season. A 1046 mb Arctic high over Quebec is wedging cold
air down the east side of the Appalachians, so the thermal contrast
across the Southeast is the strongest any of us has seen.

All guidance agrees on landfall between Apalachicola and Keaton Beach
around 06Z, including the 12Z GFS, ECMWF, AIFS, RRFS, and the HAFS
runs being produced for this system as a special request. We favor
St. Marks, near the middle of the envelope, at 04Z to 07Z. The 12Z
RRFS and REFS bottom the low out at 936 to 943 mb near 03Z, and the
AI guidance is only a few mb weaker. We are forecasting 939 mb at the
minimum and 941 mb at landfall.

The wind forecast is the headline. The strongest winds will be east
and southeast of the center, where the bent-back front wraps around
the south side of the low. Over the Big Bend coast and the eastern
Florida counties, 925 mb winds of 95 to 110 kt are forecast tonight
as the low comes ashore. Momentum transfer will be efficient in the
convective bands and in the dry slot behind the bent-back front.
Expect gusts of 100 to 125 mph from Perry and Keaton Beach to Madison
and Valdosta from about 11 PM tonight through 4 AM Thursday.
Sustained winds will mostly be 60 to 85 mph there, which is lower
than a hurricane producing the same gusts, but the gusts are what
will take down trees and power lines. Tallahassee will be close to
the center during the early morning hours and may get a lull of 30 to
60 minutes before the winds come back from the west and southwest. Do
not go outside during the lull. Peak gusts in Tallahassee are
forecast near 110 mph, mainly after sunrise Thursday.

A second, separate wind maximum is likely Thursday morning over
south-central Georgia. As the low occludes and turns north, a sting
jet descending out of the cloud head should reach the surface from
Moultrie and Quitman to Valdosta and Lakeland between about 5 AM and
9 AM EST. The RRFS shows it as a narrow, 20- to 40-mile-wide streak
of 90-plus kt winds at 850 mb with subsidence and drying below the
cloud head. Gusts of 100 to 125 mph are possible under it. Sting jets
are hard to pin down, so treat the location as uncertain by 30 to 50
miles.

Ahead of the low, a strongly forced squall line with embedded
supercells will race east across the Big Bend between 4 PM and 9 PM
this evening. MLCAPE of 500 to 1000 J/kg sits under 0-1 km SRH of 400
to 600 m2/s2 and effective shear over 80 kt. That is enough for
damaging gusts of 70 to 90 mph in the line and a few tornadoes,
especially in the southeast Big Bend.

Surge: south and southeast winds east of the center will pile water
into Apalachee Bay and the Big Bend coast as the low approaches. The
shallow shelf there amplifies surge efficiently, and this low's wind
field is far larger than a hurricane's. Peak inundation will occur
near and just after landfall, between 2 AM and 6 AM EST, close to the
4:51 AM high tide at Cedar Key. The highest values, 10 to 15 feet
above ground, are expected from Keaton Beach to Steinhatchee. This is
a life-threatening situation. Anyone still in Dixie, Taylor,
Jefferson, or Wakulla County evacuation zones must leave before dark.

Cold air wraps in quickly behind the low Thursday. Temperatures in
the low 70s ahead of the line will fall into the 40s across the
western half of the area by Thursday afternoon, with wind chills in
the 30s.

.SHORT TERM...
(Thursday night through Friday night)
Issued at 342 PM EST Wed Dec 16 2026

The low makes a slow cyclonic loop over southwest Georgia Thursday
night and Friday as it becomes vertically stacked under the cutoff
upper low. It will fill slowly, from about 955 mb Thursday evening to
965 mb Friday afternoon. Winds will drop off from their peak, but
this is a long-duration event. Gusts of 50 to 70 mph will continue
across south Georgia and the Big Bend through Friday, and 40 to 55
mph in the Panhandle. Additional trees will fall in saturated soils
long after the peak winds pass. Wraparound rain bands will keep rain
going over southwest Georgia and the Panhandle. Highs will only reach
the mid 40s to around 50 Friday. Lows Friday night will be in the low
to mid 30s with a raw north wind.

.LONG TERM...
(Saturday through Tuesday)
Issued at 342 PM EST Wed Dec 16 2026

The low exits east off the Georgia coast Saturday. Winds will
diminish, and drier air and sunshine will return Saturday afternoon.
Recovery conditions will be cold: lows in the upper 20s to low 30s
Sunday and Monday mornings, with a freeze likely north of I-10. Many
people will still be without power, so plan for heat safely and never
run generators indoors. Rivers will keep rising into early next week.

Conditions deteriorate from west to east this afternoon as the squall
line arrives. Expect TSRA with gusts of 50 to 70 kt at ECP after 21Z,
TLH and ABY after 23Z, and VLD after 01Z. Behind the line, south to
southeast winds of 35 to 50 kt with gusts of 80 to 105 kt are

10Z and 14Z. Low-level wind shear is extreme throughout. Airport
operations at TLH and VLD will likely be suspended overnight. IFR to
LIFR in +RA continues through Thursday morning.

.MARINE...
Issued at 342 PM EST Wed Dec 16 2026

A Hurricane Force Wind Warning is in effect for all waters tonight
into Thursday, with south to southwest winds of 65 to 85 kt and seas
of 25 to 35 ft offshore. Winds turn west and northwest Thursday and
stay at storm force into Friday. This is a dangerous, potentially
life-threatening marine event. All vessels should already be in port.

.HYDROLOGY...
Issued at 342 PM EST Wed Dec 16 2026

WPC has a Moderate Risk (level 3 of 4) of excessive rainfall through
7 AM Thursday, and a High Risk (level 4 of 4) from 7 AM Thursday to 7
AM Friday from Dothan and Albany through Tallahassee and Valdosta.
October through mid-December rainfall has run 175 to 250 percent of
normal under the El Nino pattern, soils are saturated, and 3-hour
flash flood guidance is down to 1.5 to 2.5 inches. Storm totals of 6
to 10 inches, locally 12 near Valdosta, are forecast through Friday,
with much of it falling Thursday as the low loops over southwest
Georgia and the cloud-head band pivots in place. Flash flooding is
likely in Tallahassee and Valdosta. The Ochlockonee, Aucilla,
Withlacoochee, and Little Rivers are forecast to reach moderate to
major flood stage this weekend. Coastal flooding will block outflow
on the lower Ochlockonee and St. Marks Rivers through Thursday.
