Day 2 Convective Outlook
NWS Storm Prediction Center Norman OK
1129 AM CST Tue Dec 15 2026

Valid 161200Z - 171200Z

...THERE IS AN ENHANCED RISK OF SEVERE THUNDERSTORMS ACROSS THE
SOUTHEAST FLORIDA BIG BEND AND THE NORTH-CENTRAL FLORIDA PENINSULA...

...SUMMARY...
A fast-moving squall line with embedded supercells is expected to
cross the Florida Big Bend and the northern and central Florida
peninsula Wednesday afternoon through Wednesday night. Damaging gusts
are the primary threat, with locally 75+ mph gusts where the line
bows. A few tornadoes are also likely, and one or two could be
strong.

...Synopsis...
An exceptionally intense extratropical cyclone is forecast to develop
over the Gulf on Wednesday. A compact northern-stream shortwave
dropping through the southern Plains will phase with an extremely
strong subtropical jet. That jet is running well above 180 kt at 250
mb along the northern Gulf coast, consistent with the ongoing record
El Nino. The resulting surface low, near 1003 mb over the western
Gulf this morning, is forecast to deepen explosively as it lifts
northeast over unusually warm Gulf waters. Most 12Z guidance takes it
below 950 mb by early Thursday as it approaches the Big Bend coast. A
1045+ mb Arctic high over Quebec will hold a strong cold-air-damming
wedge east of the Appalachians, so the warm sector will stay confined
to Florida and the immediate Big Bend and southeast Georgia coast.

...Florida Big Bend and Peninsula...
Ahead of the deepening low, rich Gulf moisture will surge north, with
surface dewpoints of 68 to 72 F reaching the Big Bend and the
northern peninsula by Wednesday afternoon. Mid-level lapse rates will
be poor, generally 5.5 to 6 C/km, so MLCAPE will be limited to 500 to
1200 J/kg. The kinematic environment will be extreme. A 70 to 90 kt
southerly low-level jet will produce 0-1 km SRH of 400 to 600 m2/s2,
with effective shear of 70 to 90 kt.

A strongly forced squall line is expected to organize over the
northeast Gulf by midday Wednesday. It should reach the Big Bend
coast by mid-afternoon, then cross the northern and central peninsula
at 45 to 55 kt through the evening and overnight. The 12Z RRFS and
most HREF members show a continuous QLCS with embedded supercell
structures and bowing segments. Given the strength of the low-level
wind field, damaging gusts appear likely along much of the line, and
gusts above 75 mph are possible with bowing segments. Line-normal 0-3
km shear near 50 kt and very large low-level SRH favor QLCS
mesovortices. Any discrete or semi-discrete supercells ahead of the
line, most likely over the north-central peninsula during the
evening, would pose a risk of a strong tornado.

The Enhanced Risk covers the corridor where the strongest forcing
overlaps the best low-level moisture, from the southeast Big Bend
through Gainesville and Ocala to the Orlando area. Instability will
drop off farther north into south Georgia, but the line may still
produce damaging gusts there before it weakens over the
cold-air-damming wedge. Farther south, the line will likely weaken
late Wednesday night as the forcing lifts north, though isolated
damaging gusts and a brief tornado remain possible into south
Florida.

Separately, the synoptic-scale wind field around this low will
produce widespread damaging non-convective winds across the Big Bend
and south Georgia Wednesday night and Thursday. That threat is
covered by local NWS offices and is not reflected in these
probabilities.
