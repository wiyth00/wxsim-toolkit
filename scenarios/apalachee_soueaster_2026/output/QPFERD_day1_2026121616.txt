...16Z Update...

...Florida Big Bend, South Georgia, and the Southeast Coastal
Plain...

The inherited Slight Risk was upgraded to a Moderate Risk from the
eastern Florida Panhandle across the Big Bend and south Georgia to
near Savannah after coordination with WFOs TAE and JAX. The Slight
Risk was expanded northeast along the coastal plain to the South
Carolina Lowcountry. An extraordinarily intense extratropical low
over the eastern Gulf, near 962 mb at 15Z and still deepening
rapidly, is forecast to make landfall near St. Marks around 06Z at
940 to 945 mb. It will then slow and begin a cyclonic loop over
southwest Georgia that continues through Day 2.

Two rain regimes overlap in the Moderate Risk. First, a strongly
forced squall line with embedded supercells crosses the Big Bend and
the Florida peninsula this afternoon and evening, with brief rates of
2 to 3 inches per hour. Second, the warm conveyor belt wraps into the
cloud head overnight. PWs of 1.7 to 1.9 inches are forecast, near the
daily record for mid-December and about 250 percent of normal. Strong
isentropic ascent over the coastal front will support a long-lived
band of 1 to 1.5 inch per hour rates from the Big Bend to the
Okefenokee and Waycross between 03Z and 12Z. The 12Z REFS and HREF
both show neighborhood probabilities of 5 inches in 6 hours above 40
percent there. The 12Z RRFS, GFS, ECMWF and ECMWF-AIFS all have storm
totals of 6 to 10 inches across the Moderate Risk by the end of Day
2, with local amounts to 12 inches near Valdosta and Waycross.

Mid-December flash flood guidance on the sandy soils of the coastal
plain is higher than in the warm season, mostly 3 to 4 inches in 3
hours. Still, the expected rates and duration are enough to exceed it
in many places, and in Tallahassee, Valdosta and Thomasville urban
runoff will be efficient. Wind-driven coastal flooding will also
block drainage on the tidal reaches of the St. Marks, Aucilla,
Econfina and Steinhatchee Rivers through 12Z. Downed trees and power
outages will slow response and make flooded roads harder to see
overnight. Numerous instances of flash flooding are expected, some of
them significant.

Over the Florida peninsula, the squall line will produce 1 to 2
inches in an hour this evening, but its forward speed should hold
totals to 1 to 3 inches, below guidance outside of urban areas.
