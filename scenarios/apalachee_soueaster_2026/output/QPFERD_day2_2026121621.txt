...Southwest Georgia, the Florida Big Bend, and Southeast Alabama...

A High Risk has been introduced for Day 2, centered from Dothan and
Albany through Thomasville, Tallahassee and Valdosta to the
Okefenokee, in coordination with WFOs TAE and JAX and the Southeast
River Forecast Center. This is the first wintertime High Risk for
this part of the Southeast, and it reflects both the rainfall
forecast and how little the ground can take.

After making landfall in the Big Bend around 06Z Thursday near 941
mb, the low is forecast to slow to a crawl and make a cyclonic loop
over southwest Georgia through Friday as it becomes vertically
stacked beneath the cutoff upper low. The comma head and wraparound
bands will pivot over the same counties for 24 to 36 hours. The 12Z
REFS, HREF, RRFS, GFS, ECMWF and ECMWF-AIFS all have 4 to 7 inches in
the Day 2 window inside the High Risk, on top of 2 to 5 inches
expected before 12Z Thursday. Storm totals of 8 to 12 inches are
likely near Valdosta and Waycross, with 6 to 9 inches common
elsewhere. Hourly rates of 1 to 1.5 inches are expected in the
cloud-head band Thursday, with training.

Antecedent conditions are the reason for the High Risk. The record El
Nino has sent repeated subtropical-jet systems across the Gulf Coast
since October. October through mid-December rainfall is 175 to 250
percent of normal across the region, soils are near saturation, and
the Ochlockonee, Withlacoochee, Flint and Alapaha were already
running above normal. Low winter evaporation means there has been
little recovery between events. Three-hour flash flood guidance is
1.5 to 2.5 inches across most of the area, and will drop below 1 inch
where Wednesday night's rain verifies. Widespread wind damage will
also leave culverts and drainage blocked by fallen trees, and coastal
flooding will slow drainage on the tidal reaches of the Big Bend
rivers. Widespread, life-threatening flash flooding is expected,
along with major river flooding that will continue well beyond this
period.

...Florida Panhandle, Southeast Georgia, and the South Carolina
Lowcountry...

The warm conveyor belt continues to wrap around the east side of the
low Thursday, bringing additional 2 to 4 inch totals from the
Okefenokee to Savannah and the Lowcountry. Totals there will be lower
than in the High Risk, but guidance is similarly low on wet soils.
Coastal flooding at high tide will make drainage worse around
Savannah, Beaufort and Charleston. North of the Moderate Risk, the
precipitation falls as freezing rain and snow and is not included in
this outlook.
